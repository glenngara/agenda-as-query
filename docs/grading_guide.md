# Grading guide given to human graders (verbatim content, contact details removed)

**What the study is about.** The Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (PBSAP) 2024 to 2040 has 23
national biodiversity goals. We want to know how much scientific research exists for each goal. We collected
17,242 papers on Philippine ecology and environment (2005 to 2025). For each goal, computer search tools picked
20 papers that may match, giving 460 goal-and-paper pairs. AI (Claude and GPT) also grades these pairs.

**Roles.** Lead grader: all 460 pairs, then a repeat check of 60 pairs after a break. Second graders: 60
selected pairs, spread across all 23 goals (about 2 to 3 per goal).

**How to grade.** Each screen shows one goal (title, description and action list) and one paper (title, year
and abstract). Ask: *would this paper help someone working on this goal?*

- **3, highly relevant:** directly about the goal and could help meet or monitor it.
- **2, relevant:** on topic, but only partly or indirectly.
- **1, marginal:** shares some words, but would not really help.
- **0, not relevant:** not related to the goal.

**Rules.**
1. Grade how related the paper is to the goal, not how good the paper is.
2. Use only the title and abstract. Do not look up the full paper or the authors.
3. A study outside the Philippines can get a 2. Give 3 mainly to Philippine studies or studies that clearly
   apply to the Philippines.
4. If unsure, type a short note first, then pick your best grade.
5. Grade on your own. Do not discuss specific papers with other graders, and do not look at the AI grades,
   until everyone is done.
6. For the repeat check, do not look back at your first grades. Grade each pair fresh.
